\# Pilot Group Design



\## ACME Intune Rollout Plan



The rollout will be completed over four weeks and include all 50 ACME endpoints. A phased deployment reduces risk and allows issues to be identified before affecting the entire organization. This approach also gives the IT team time to validate enrollment procedures, confirm policy delivery, and provide user support before larger groups are migrated.



\### Wave 1



Wave 1 will include IT staff and technical users. These users are the lowest-risk group because they can troubleshoot minor issues and provide detailed feedback. Approximately five devices will be enrolled during this phase. Any enrollment issues can be identified and corrected before additional users are affected.



\### Wave 2



Wave 2 will include administrative and office staff from the primary location. These users represent normal business workflows and provide a good test of daily operations, including access to shared resources, Microsoft 365 applications, and company policies.



\### Wave 3



Wave 3 will include department managers and supervisors. By this stage, the enrollment process should be validated and support documentation should be complete. Management users are included after the process has demonstrated stability.



\### Wave 4



Wave 4 will include all remaining users, remote workers, and any systems with specialized business applications. This group has the highest business impact and will only be enrolled after the previous waves are stable and fully documented.



\## Advancement Criteria



Each wave must demonstrate successful enrollment into Microsoft Entra ID and Intune. Devices must appear in the Intune portal, show a recent check-in, report a healthy management state, and generate no unresolved support tickets for at least 48 hours before moving to the next wave.



\## Rollback Signal



The rollout should be paused if more than 20% of devices in a wave fail enrollment, do not appear in Intune, lose access to required business resources, or experience policy-related disruptions. Any widespread issue affecting productivity should be investigated and resolved before proceeding to the next wave.
